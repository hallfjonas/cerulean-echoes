David Attenborough narrates Beats & Basteln

TERATION 2 · AMBIENT NARRATION · 7–8 MINUTES

A calm, intimate voice. Slowly. Almost imperceptibly curious.

There are places on Earth [Pause] where the boundaries between the natural world and the imagination become remarkably difficult to distinguish.

And here, beneath the restless autumn skies of Berlin, we find a particularly fascinating gathering of nocturnal creatures.

[Pause. A faint breath.]

As the evening progresses, something rather unusual is taking place.

The creatures have begun their annual ritual of illumination.

With extraordinary precision, they gather fragments of wood, delicate sheets of coloured paper, and mysterious objects of unknown origin. These materials are assembled into elaborate structures which appear to serve no obvious biological purpose.

[Slightly amused.]

And yet, their significance appears to be profound.

For generations, these creatures have been carrying these small artificial suns through the darkness. Perhaps in an attempt to communicate with distant stars. Perhaps simply to remind themselves that darkness, after all, is not an entirely permanent condition.

[Long pause.]
[Chapter Ends; Page Turns]

Further away, beneath the surface of the great ocean, another ceremony is underway.

The whales are singing.

Their voices travel across immense distances, passing through layers of salt, pressure and forgotten memories.

There is something almost incomprehensible about these sounds. A sequence of low-frequency pulses, elongated tones and delicate harmonic variations, unfolding over enormous stretches of time.

Scientists have discovered that even the most convincing approximation of a whale's voice can emerge from remarkably simple ingredients.

A sine wave. A triangle wave. A carefully controlled modulation of frequency. Perhaps a little pitch drift, some filtered noise, and a generous amount of reverberation.

[With quiet enthusiasm.]

And suddenly, a creature that weighs forty tonnes appears to be communicating from the other side of the universe.

The secret, it seems, is not necessarily complexity.

It is movement. Space. Resonance.

And the occasional sound effect that makes absolutely no sense until one closes one's eyes.

[Pause. Softer, almost whispering.]
[Chapter Ends; Page Turns]

Somewhere in the distance, a siren begins to sing.

In Greek mythology, these extraordinary beings were known to lure unsuspecting sailors towards the rocks through the use of irresistible melodies.

Their songs were said to contain something more powerful than beauty.

An invitation.

A temporary suspension of ordinary perception.

A suggestion that the world might be considerably larger than the one we normally inhabit.

Today, their influence appears to have extended far beyond the Mediterranean.

In certain underground habitats of Berlin, creatures can be observed responding to similarly hypnotic frequencies.

Unlike their maritime ancestors, however, they do not appear particularly interested in the destruction of ships.

Their attention is directed towards the delicate relationship between rhythm, repetition and collective movement.

[A slight smile in the voice.]

An interesting development.

The ocean, it seems, has found its way into the city.

And the city, in turn, has begun to imitate the ocean.

[Pause. Let the music breathe.]

There is a particular quality to these environments when the distinction between individual sounds begins to dissolve.

Wind becomes texture.

Rain becomes percussion.

A distant flame becomes an irregular sequence of transients.

And the wooden structures surrounding these creatures introduce subtle resonances, absorbing some frequencies while allowing others to travel freely through the air.

The result is an acoustic environment of extraordinary depth.

One might almost describe it as an expansion of consciousness.

Not a dramatic transformation. Nothing particularly overwhelming.

More like discovering that the familiar room in which one has been standing for several hours possesses an additional dimension.

[Pause.]

A rather peculiar sensation.

The creatures appear to become increasingly attentive to sounds that would ordinarily escape their perception.

A tiny variation in pitch.

A barely perceptible change in density.

The space between two consecutive pulses.

And occasionally, an ecstatic response to a sound that, to the untrained observer, might appear to be little more than a distant electrical disturbance.

[A little more animated.]

There is, of course, an extraordinary degree of cooperation involved.

One creature introduces a pulse.

Another responds with a sequence of irregular percussive signals.

A third introduces a continuous atmospheric layer, creating an acoustic environment that resembles the interior of a particularly large seashell.

The sounds begin to overlap.

They expand.

They contract.

They develop their own internal weather systems.

And, gradually, something emerges that none of the individual creatures could have produced alone.

[Pause. Lower the voice.]

It is worth observing that EDM represents a rather unfortunate interruption of this delicate ecological balance. Its relentless insistence on spectacle and predictability is best left to considerably less discerning species.

[Long pause. Let a low drone emerge.]

But perhaps the most remarkable aspect of this gathering is the coexistence of seemingly unrelated rituals.

In one chamber, creatures are constructing small illuminated ecosystems from paper and wood.

In another, they are attempting to recreate the atmospheric conditions of distant oceans through sound.

Neither activity appears to serve an immediate evolutionary advantage.

And yet, both are driven by the same instinct.

The desire to transform ordinary materials into something extraordinary.

[Very slowly.]

A piece of paper becomes a vessel for light.

A simple waveform becomes the voice of an ancient marine giant.

A sequence of repeated sounds becomes a place in which several creatures can temporarily lose their sense of individual boundaries.

Perhaps this is what the sirens understood all along.

That the most extraordinary journeys do not always require movement.

Sometimes, one only needs to listen.

[Pause. A little wonder in the voice.]

And there are, inevitably, certain risks.

Occasionally, a creature becomes so absorbed in the manipulation of frequencies that it forgets the passage of time.

Another may become convinced that a particular filter setting has fundamentally altered the structure of the universe.

Such behaviours are not uncommon.

They are generally harmless.

And, in certain circumstances, they appear to be positively ecstatic.

[A gentle, knowing tone.]

As the evening continues, the rain settles over Berlin.

The ocean remains restless.

Somewhere beneath its surface, the whales continue their conversation, their enormous voices travelling through the darkness.

The sirens, too, remain at their stations.

And here, in this curious gathering of terrestrial creatures, the lanterns continue to glow.

[Long pause. Almost a whisper.]

Perhaps the creatures are not attempting to escape the natural world.

Perhaps they are simply discovering that they have never been separate from it.

The rhythm was always there.

In the movement of water.

In the vibration of wood.

In the irregular breathing of fire.

In the distant calls of animals that inhabit places we may never visit.

[Pause.]

And sometimes, when the conditions are just right, all of these sounds seem to converge.

Not into a melody.

Not into a song.

But into something much more difficult to describe.

[Very softly.]

A shared moment.

A small expansion.

A temporary alignment of otherwise unrelated things.

[Long pause.]

For now, the creatures remain undisturbed.

The lanterns are glowing.

The whales are singing.

And somewhere, in the distance, a siren produces a note that seems to contain the entire history of the natural world.

[A final, almost imperceptible smile.]

They have found their rhythm.

And, perhaps more importantly...

They have found one another.

NARRATION & SOUND DESIGN
- Voice: Deep, warm, measured British nature-documentary delivery.
- Pacing: Leave 3–6 seconds at marked pauses. Occasionally longer.
- Processing: Subtle tape saturation, long dark reverb (3–5 seconds), gentle stereo widening.
- Positioning: Relatively low in the mix, as though transmitted from another room.
- Evolution: Gradually introduce more spaciousness and reverberation towards the final third. Occasionally filter the voice as if it were slowly disappearing beneath the ocean.
 The listener should occasionally wonder whether they actually heard what they think they heard.
