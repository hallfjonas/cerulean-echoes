# Research stabs (2026-11-08) — sources, candidates (not listened to, only title/length/licence/CORS checked)

## What, where, how
- Phrases: 8/16/32 bars; small changes every 8, big ones every 16; fills at the end of an 8/16 block, not every 2 bars
  (gearspace.com/threads/house-music-structure.956852/; dj.studio/blog/phrasing-dj-mixing).
- Vocal shouts ("Yeah! Woo!", Lyn Collins "Think", 2nd most sampled track; attackmagazine …/the-2nd-most-sampled-track-in-history/).
  Best one-liners from ad-libs, little delay in classic vocal house (magneticmag.com …/5-tips-for-perfecting-house-vocals…).
- Spoken word (Chuck Roberts "My House" over "Can You Feel It"; Pépé Bradock "Deep Burnt") — rather in breaks (conclusion).
- Orchestra hit: gets cheesy quickly (Wikipedia Orchestra_hit).
- Ear candy: short, mostly high, separate from the drum track, surprises the first time (audient.com/tutorial/ear-candy-production-tips/;
  musicradar.com/how-to/ear-candy-daw-music-production-tricks).
- Echo throw: send only for the last word of a phrase, dotted 1/8 (attackmagazine creating-dub-delays…; musicguymixing.com/delay-throw/).
- Flute/whistle/cuíca/glissandi: Latin/garage tradition, no documented placement rule.
- Placement (conclusion): pickup on the "and" of 4 in bar 8/16 or an answer on the 1 of the new phrase; at most 1× per 8 bars,
  often 1× per 16, ~50 % chance to skip. Rare = may be louder; own register (HPF 300–500 Hz), dry + one echo throw,
  duck the keys briefly (duckorbit).
