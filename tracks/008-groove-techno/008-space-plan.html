008 Space Map

A plan for where each stem lives in the mix, how we measure it, and how the bassline gets a lot more room. Nothing in the track is changed yet: this is the plan to test first.

What “space” means here

A mix is like a room you look into. Every sound gets a place in it. There are three directions, plus time. Two sounds that sit on the same spot in all of them hide each other (this is called masking).

Low ↔ highThe pitch range (frequency). Sub and kick at the bottom, hats at the top.
Left ↔ rightThe pan. In Strudel pan(0) = left, 0.5 = middle, 1 = right.
Near ↔ farThe depth. Dry, loud and bright = near. Reverb, quiet and dull = far.
TimeWhich 16th a sound plays on. Two low sounds can share a pitch range if they take turns.

What the code shows today

  • The low end is crowded. Between 125 and 350 Hz six stems play at once: rumble (its reverb tail), bassline, stab (its square one octave down sits exactly on the bass), pad, techno pad and the Launchkey pad. Pad and techno pad have no high-pass filter at all.
  • The bassline is small. It is mono, in the middle, and at brightness 0 its filter sits at 200 Hz. A dull sound reads as far away, and on small speakers 87 Hz notes are barely there.
  • The bassline and the rumble take the same 16ths. 8 of the 12 notes of the rolling line land on a rumble hit, and the loudest bass accent (the 3rd 16th) is a rumble slot too.
  • The left and right edges are almost empty. Most stems sit between pan 0.35 and 0.65. Only the answer, the bongos and the Launchkey pad reach out. Pad and techno pad move, but all their voices move together, so they stay narrow.
  • Depth is mostly fine. Drums and bass are on orbit 1 (small room), all of Jonas’ colour on orbit 2 (big dub room), rumble on its own orbit 3. We keep this.

The bassline gets more room

Six moves. Each one is small and can be tested alone. Together they make the bassline the biggest shape in the front half of the mix.

Clear the bass lane

High-pass the sounds that do not need the 125–350 Hz range: pad at 250 Hz, techno pad at 220 Hz, Launchkey pad from 140 to 220 Hz. The stab loses its square one octave down (that square plays at F2, exactly the bass note) or gets a high-pass at 280 Hz.

stemN1_pad: stack(...).hpf(250)     stemN2_tpad: stack(...).hpf(220)
stemN4_stab: drop stabNotes.add(note(-12)).s('square')   // or .hpf(280)
lkPad: .hpf(220)
Put the rumble under the bass

The rumble becomes the floor (40–150 Hz) and stops spreading up into the bass lane. Lower its filter range and its reverb range a little.

rumble lpf: expRange(rumble.bright, 250, 1800)  →  expRange(rumble.bright, 150, 700)
rumble room: 0.5 + 0.5 * s  →  0.35 + 0.45 * s
Make the bassline wide on top, mono at the bottom

Split the bassline in two. The core (below 300 Hz) stays mono in the middle, so the low end stays tight in clubs and in mono. A new top layer plays the same notes, high-passed at 300 Hz, as two slightly detuned copies: one left (0.3), one right (0.7). This is where the new width comes from.

const bassTop = (pat, detune, side) => bassSound(pat).add(note(detune)).hpf(300).pan(side)
stack(
  bassSound(line).lpf(300),                    // core: mono, middle
  bassTop(line, 0.08, 0.3), bassTop(line, -0.08, 0.7)   // top: wide
).gain(...)
Bring it to the front

Brightness 0 starts at 350 Hz instead of 200 Hz, so the bass is heard on small speakers and sounds near. It stays dry on orbit 1. Level range 0.5 → 0.7 (about +3 dB).

lpf: expRange(bassCol.bright, 200, 2000)  →  expRange(bassCol.bright, 350, 2500)
level: bassCol.level.range(0, 0.5)  →  bassCol.level.range(0, 0.7)
Give the bass its own 16ths

Same pitch at the same time is where masking hurts most. Two options to try at rehearsal, see picture 3: (a) keep the rhythm, the split in moves 1–3 already separates them by pitch; (b) when the rolling line plays, Jojo picks rumble pattern “3” (only the 3rd 16th): the overlap falls from 8 to 4 notes.

Duck the pads a little on bass notes optional

Only if moves 1–5 are not enough: the techno pad already pumps with the kick (its row 3). The same idea, a small dip on the bass accents, would make the bass punch through. Costs CPU, so last.

Every stem, placed

The target for each stem. Stems the rehearsal feedback called good (sub, clap, bongos, answer) keep their sound. The pan and pitch numbers are what the code should do; the KPIs check the result.

Side effect to know: the global Thin knob multiplies each stem’s own high-pass. A stem that gets an own high-pass (pads at 250 Hz) thins out sooner when Thin goes up. Thin stays capped at 5 kHz, so nothing goes silent.

Steps

  1. Build a space meter: tools/strudel-sim/space.mjs, next to measure.mjs. It renders each stem alone in stereo (render.mjs already renders 2 channels) and prints, per octave band: level, left/right balance, side vs. middle energy, and the reverb share. Check: the kick shows as mono and centred.
  2. Measure today’s mix and write the numbers into the KPI cards. All knobs at 0, all faders up, chapter dark. This is the baseline.
  3. Make a lab board round in lab/bass/ with three columns: A = moves 1–2 (clear the lane), B = A + move 3 (wide top), C = B + move 4 (front + level). Play each against the kick, rumble and pads. Jojo picks a winner; the result goes into lab/bass/feedback.md.
  4. Put the winner and the other placements into a new file 008-groove-techno-space.strudel. The current track stays as it is. Prove with /strudel-optimize that the stems we did not touch sound identical.
  5. Measure again and compare with the baseline. Every KPI should reach its goal.
  6. Rehearsal check: headphones, then a phone speaker, then mono. The ear tests in the KPI cards are the checklist.

Open questions

  • Is the rumble’s wide reverb tail part of the sound you want? KPI 1 (mono low end) will likely fail because of it. The plan narrows it, but a fully mono rumble would sound different.
  • The wide top layer adds 2 saw voices per bass note. If the M2 drops out, the top copies lose their own detune (superimpose) first.
  • Should the stab keep a bit of low weight? If removing its square makes it thin, a high-pass at 280 Hz is the softer option.
